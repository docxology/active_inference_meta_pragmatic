# Related Work {#sec:related_work}

This section situates our framework within five intersecting research traditions: Active Inference foundations, meta-cognition in cognitive science, predictive processing, AI safety and value alignment, and cognitive security.

## Active Inference Foundations

The Free Energy Principle, proposed by \cite{friston2010free}, provides the variational foundation for Active Inference, positing that biological systems maintain their organization by minimizing variational free energy. Subsequent work formalized Active Inference on discrete state-spaces \cite{dacosta2020active}, established the epistemic-pragmatic decomposition of Expected Free Energy \cite{friston2015active}, and demonstrated connections to homeostatic regulation \cite{pezzulo2015active}. The comprehensive textbook by \cite{parr2022active} consolidated these developments into a unified treatment.

Recent advances extend the foundational framework in important directions. Scale-free formulations bridge discrete and continuous state-spaces \cite{friston2025scalefree}, while EFE-based planning has been reformulated as principled variational inference over policies \cite{champion2025efeplanning}. Integrations with projective simulation offer interpretable active inference implementations \cite{feps2025}.

Applied systems demonstrate the practical reach of these foundations in artificial agents. The MetaMind architecture implements metacognitive multi-agent systems for social reasoning \cite{metamind2025}, and the SOFAI framework embodies fast-slow-metacognitive processing in artificial intelligence \cite{sofai2025}. Our work extends this tradition by examining the meta-level implications of generative model specification---a dimension largely implicit in prior treatments.

## Contrast with Reinforcement Learning

A foundational differentiator of our framework is its stark contrast with traditional reinforcement learning (RL). In canonical RL paradigms, reward functions $R(s,a)$ are extrinsically imposed, opaque scalars, reducing the agent's burden to mere downstream cumulative reward maximization \cite{tschantz2020scaling}. Active Inference subverts this by internalizing the objective function as the preference prior $C$ embedded entirely within the generative model. This architectural shift enables multidimensional value landscapes, intrinsic epistemic drives, and---crucially---the radical possibility of treating the ontological value specification itself as a primary, inspectable research variable. Our quadrant framework mathematically formalizes this meta-level structure, opening pathways to analyze how agents might recursively evaluate their own preference parameters.

## Meta-Cognition in Cognitive Science

Meta-cognition---cognition about cognition---was first systematically studied by \cite{flavell1979metacognition}, who distinguished meta-cognitive knowledge from meta-cognitive regulation. \cite{nelson1990metamemory} formalized the monitoring-control framework distinguishing object-level processing from meta-level oversight, a distinction that maps directly onto our Cognitive/Meta-Cognitive axis. More recently, \cite{fleming2012metacognition} developed computational models of meta-cognitive sensitivity, demonstrating that confidence calibration is itself a measurable cognitive capacity. Metacognitive architectures have been implemented in AI systems, including MetaMind for social reasoning \cite{metamind2025} and the SofAI framework for fast-slow-metacognitive thinking \cite{sofai2025}. Our quadrant framework formalizes these distinctions within the Active Inference formalism, providing mathematical specificity to the monitoring-control hierarchy.

Computational psychiatry has leveraged precision-weighted inference to explain clinical phenomena at the level of generative model parameters. In this framework, delusions arise from miscalibrated precision weighting on prior beliefs, implemented as an abnormally high confidence placed on matrix $D$ relative to sensory evidence from matrix $A$ \cite{friston2014active}. Such applications underscore the clinical relevance of explicit generative model parameterization: diagnostic targets become identifiable model parameters rather than opaque symptoms.

## Predictive Processing

The predictive processing paradigm \cite{clark2013whatever} proposes that brains are prediction machines that minimize prediction error through hierarchical generative models. \cite{hohwy2013predictive} developed this into a comprehensive theory of perception, action, and cognition, emphasizing the role of precision-weighting in hierarchical inference. Active Inference inherits and extends predictive processing by adding action selection through EFE minimization \cite{friston2015active}. Our Data/Meta-Data axis captures a dimension that predictive processing theorists have discussed informally---the distinction between processing sensory data and processing information *about* sensory reliability---and formalizes it within the generative model framework.

## AI Safety and Value Alignment

The value alignment problem---ensuring autonomous systems pursue objectives genuinely aligned with complex human values---has been articulated by \cite{russell2019human} as the existential challenge for advanced artificial intelligence. \cite{amodei2016concrete} catalogued concrete safety pathologies including reward hacking, scalable oversight bottlenecks, and fatal distributional shifts. Active Inference offers a structurally distinctive approach to these pathologies: rather than attempting to hand-code leak-proof scalar reward functions, the modeler engineers inspectable, interpretable preference priors ($C$ matrix) tightly constrained by explicit epistemic structures ($A$, $B$, $D$ matrices) \cite{sajid2022active}. The meta-pragmatic perspective developed here radicalizes this insight, demonstrating that rigorous framework specification provides a principled foundation for value alignment through the explicit mathematical parameterization of precisely what matters and what can be known.

## Cognitive Security

Cognitive security addresses the protection of cognitive processes from adversarial manipulation. \cite{wardle2017information} developed influential frameworks for understanding information disorder including misinformation, disinformation, and malinformation. \cite{benkler2018network} analyzed how networked information ecosystems create vulnerabilities in collective sense-making. In the Active Inference context, \cite{constant2019regimes} examined how cultural affordances and epistemic niches shape the generative models through which agents interpret the world. Our contribution maps these concerns onto the quadrant framework, showing that different types of cognitive manipulation target different quadrants---from sensory data manipulation (Q1) to framework-level subversion (Q4)---and that defense strategies must operate at corresponding meta-levels.

The quadrant framework proposed here organises these developments into a unified taxonomy by asking a single structural question: does the processing occur at the level of data or meta-data, and does it employ cognitive or meta-cognitive mechanisms?
