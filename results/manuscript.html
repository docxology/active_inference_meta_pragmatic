Active Inference as a Meta-Pragmatic Framework

Daniel Ari Friedman

Working manuscript · Synthetic computational demonstration

Abstract

Active inference makes beliefs and policies conditional on a generative model, leaving open how its categories, evidence and purposes become warranted. We develop active inference as a meta-pragmatic framework for making these conditions of inquiry themselves available to inquiry. Four overlapping roles organize the proposal: inferring states and evaluating actions, contextualizing evidence, monitoring uncertainty and resources, and evaluating or revising model commitments. These roles distinguish responsibilities within inquiry rather than successive stages or separate cognitive faculties. In dialogue with American Pragmatism, situated cognition and Bayesian model criticism, the framework treats knowledge as answerable to consequences while keeping predictive success, explanatory understanding and the justification of ends distinct. Mathematical coherence cannot establish the adequacy of a representation, and optimization under declared preferences cannot justify those preferences. Public criticism consequently concerns both the grounds of a decision and whose experience can challenge the purposes it serves.

The proposal is made concrete through executable Generalized Notation Notation (GNN) specifications imported with the upstream reference parser and evaluated by an exact categorical runtime. Complementary synthetic studies examine source reliability, misleading metadata, confidence-triggered evidence acquisition, likelihoods fitted on separate labeled training data, and action selection by expected free energy. Paired comparisons across independent simulation replicates retain predictive scores, observation costs and uncertainty. Reliable metadata and further sensing improve classification in the specified sensor task, while misleading metadata and a separately specified world reveal how exact inference can remain confidently mistaken. The contribution is an inspectable discipline of specification, evaluation and accountable revision, supported by model diagrams, retained evidence and technical supplements. The demonstrations establish conditional computational consequences; they neither automate the justification of ends nor validate the framework's broader biological or social interpretation. Sources, model specifications and retained evidence accompany the manuscript at https://github.com/docxology/active_inference_meta_pragmatic.

The Representational Commitments That Make Inference Possible

An agent can infer correctly under an inappropriate observation model or select the minimum-cost policy under inappropriate preferences. In either case, improving the inference algorithm alone may leave the error intact. This distinction motivates examining how a model is specified, evaluated, and revised.

We use meta-epistemic for choices about the representation and acquisition of evidence, and meta-pragmatic for choices about preferences and the evaluation of action. These are interpretive terms for modeling activities. They do not imply that ordinary parameter estimation is impossible or that an agent's initial ontology is irrevocable. Observation and transition models can be learned; the selection of representations, update rules, and constraints remains a modeling decision.

Figure fig:quadrant_orientation gives the four roles a shared visual reference before their formal development. Our contribution is a taxonomy connecting these activities to inspectable computational mechanisms. We distinguish data from information about its source or reliability, and direct inference from evaluation of inference. The resulting quadrants organize examples and failure cases. Their usefulness depends on operational definitions and comparative tests; the taxonomy itself does not guarantee improved performance. A model is therefore both a device for inference and an object of criticism: its answers can be internally consistent while its assumptions remain inadequate.

The paper contributes (1) explicit definitions of the four quadrants, (2) executable Generalized Notation Notation (GNN) specifications imported through the upstream reference parser, (3) an exact categorical runtime with independent mathematical checks, (4) paired synthetic comparisons that retain evidence and account for sensing resources, and (5) a philosophical account of model criticism and responsible revision. Active inference is not the only framework in which these questions arise. Reinforcement learning, Bayesian decision theory, active learning, and meta-learning also permit learned models, structured objectives, and information-seeking behavior.

Computational, methodological, and public responsibility

We call active inference a meta-pragmatic framework when its explicit models support inquiry into the consequences of modeling and acting: which distinctions are represented, which evidence is acquired, which outcomes are preferred, and what would justify revising those commitments. The framework consists of a declared model, an executable decision procedure, an external evaluation reference, and an accountable revision process. Only the first two are supplied by an inference calculation. Meta-pragmatic practice connects all four; it is not an additional hidden optimization objective that automatically establishes the authority of its own preferences.

This interpretation follows a practical-consequence tradition while preserving differences between philosophical explanation and computational evidence. Peirce connects inquiry to unsettled belief and conceptual meaning to conceivable practical effects [1], [2]. We operationalize that orientation through interventions on model components and their evaluated consequences. Our synthetic experiments test those interventions in declared worlds; they do not demonstrate that the philosophical interpretation is uniquely correct.

At the computational level, a declared model determines what counts as evidence and which policies receive favorable scores. At the methodological level, a researcher chooses comparisons, scoring rules, budgets, and circumstances under which a model will be revised. At the normative level, someone must justify which outcomes matter and whose interests constrain revision. A successful calculation at one level does not discharge obligations at the others. Calling a process meta-epistemic or meta-pragmatic is useful only when its referent and evaluation criterion are stated.

The executable specification is the bridge between the first two levels. GNN exposes likelihoods, transitions, preference logits, priors, dimensions, and dependency declarations before execution. It makes a model inspectable and portable within a supported profile; it does not establish that the model is true or its preferences legitimate. The present implementation imports real upstream code rather than treating a diagram or a notation label as an executed model.

American Pragmatism and the Public Criticism of Models

American Pragmatism asks how inquiry earns and revises its commitments through consequences, criticism and shared experience. Its plural traditions supply distinct questions for this framework, rather than a historical derivation of the four roles or an anticipation of categorical active inference.

Peirce: doubt, habit, and public correction

Peirce distinguishes scientific inquiry, answerable to an independent reality, from tenacity, authority and the a priori method [1]. These four methods concern the settlement of belief; they do not correspond to our four intersecting roles. His pragmatic maxim clarifies a conception through conceivable practical consequences [2]; his later account connects belief, habit, surprise and self-control [3]. The modeling question is what a commitment leads us to expect and what could unsettle it. Posterior concentration alone supplies neither genuine doubt nor public correction: our misleading-metadata control shows internally coherent inference under inadequate premises.

James: verification, consequences, and the correction of truth claims

James asks what difference competing ideas make in experience, while making verification, consistency and subsequent correction central to truth claims [4], Lectures II and VI. This directs attention to predicted observations, changed actions, incurred costs and possible counterevidence. It does not identify truth with convenience or one performance score. Accuracy and proper losses reveal different consequences; their selection and evaluation horizon remain contestable commitments.

Dewey: reflective inquiry and the reconstruction of ends

For Dewey, reflection examines the grounds and consequences of belief and sustains inquiry through a problematic situation [5], Chapter I. A confidence-triggered observation rule captures only a narrow decision about continuing investigation. His treatment of ends through needs, opportunities and consequences also makes purposes available for reconstruction [6], Chapters VI--VII. Computing an action under C therefore differs from asking whether C expresses an adequate end. Our fixed evaluation criteria expose this distinction without implementing autonomous reconstruction of purposes.

Addams: whose experience can correct the evaluator?

Addams challenges the sufficiency of private virtue for social ethics and brings diverse experience into the correction of moral judgment [7], Introduction and Charitable Effort. Her question for evaluation is whose experience can contest the declared account of benefit and harm. A held-out test can assess a chosen outcome while excluding affected perspectives from its definition. Our synthetic contexts contain no social participants; participatory criticism is a normative requirement proposed for social applications, not a demonstrated software capability.

Mead: significant symbols and meaning through shared conduct

Mead situates significant symbols in coordinated social conduct and the indication of anticipated consequences [8], Sections 16 and 24 of the posthumous volume edited by Charles W. Morris. A GNN label declares an intended meaning without establishing shared interpretation. Executable specifications can make disagreement about categories precise, but our single-agent tasks do not implement symbolic interaction or the social development of a self.

What the pragmatist inheritance asks of an executable model

Together these readings ask us to articulate consequences, expose reasons, and permit criticism of representations and ends. The four roles cut across those responsibilities; no author belongs to one quadrant. In the demonstrations, the researcher specifies the world, model and evaluation. Public criticism and the social formation of ends remain outside the mechanism. A meta-pragmatic framework makes these choices inspectable without automating their justification or substituting philosophical authority for held-out evidence.

Active inference, generative models, and explicit specification

The free energy principle and discrete active inference supply the background for the distinction between state inference and model specification [9], [10]. Expected free energy balances information gain and outcome preferences under a predictive model [11]. The variational assumptions behind planning require care: expected free energy is not obtained by simply substituting future observations into the usual variational objective [12]. Our implementation is deliberately limited to categorical models with known likelihood and transition matrices.

Methods and software already address learning, hierarchical models, and practical computation [13], [14], [15]. Thus, our meta-level interpretation does not introduce model learning to active inference. Its contribution is a classification of specification and monitoring choices, coupled to explicit examples that can be checked computationally.

Recent work studies scale-free generative models, variational planning, and interpretable graph-based agents [16], [17], [18]. These are distinct technical architectures, not implementations or validation of the quadrants proposed here. We include them to clarify the surrounding research program, without importing their performance claims into our results.

OODA, active inference, and criticism across the action loop

Boyd's observe--orient--decide--act (OODA) framework provides a useful comparison for inquiry organized around action. David, Cordes and Friedman explicitly connect OODA with active inference in Active Inference in Modeling Conflict [19], especially pp. 17--18 and 37--38, Figure 9. Their alignment relates observation to incoming signals, orientation to inference about hidden causes, decision to preference-sensitive policy evaluation, and action to an enacted policy. This is a conceptual antecedent, not empirical validation of our taxonomy or experiments.

The following crosswalk is our interpretation of the present executable tasks. It distinguishes a recurring action loop from responsibilities that can operate throughout it.

\Needspace12\baselineskip

OODA function Connection to the present framework
Observe: receive evidence Q1 updates from observations; Q3 controls whether further evidence is acquired.
Orient: interpret the situation Q2 contextualizes evidence through source reliability; Q4 evaluates the assumed reliability model.
Decide: evaluate possible conduct Q1 evaluates policies under declared preferences; Q3 exposes sensing costs and stopping criteria.
Act: intervene and encounter consequences The action model's transition changes observation mode; external evaluation can then inform Q4 criticism.

The shared count of four implies no one-to-one mapping. Repeatedly executing OODA under false source assumptions can preserve a bad orientation; the misleading-metadata and world--agent mismatch controls make narrower versions of this vulnerability inspectable. Our one-step action task executes a bounded perception--action coupling, not a strategic conflict simulation. The meta-pragmatic question cuts across the loop: what evidence, representation, purpose or resource judgment warrants revision, and who may authorize it?

Metacognitive monitoring, control, and forecast calibration

The distinction between monitoring and control predates this taxonomy [20], [21]. Confidence, calibration, discrimination, and metacognitive efficiency are different quantities [22]. A concentrated posterior can be confidently wrong under model misspecification. Accordingly, the experiment reports Brier loss and log loss rather than treating posterior concentration as evidence of correctness.

MetaMind and SOFAI provide contemporary examples of metacognitive organization in artificial systems [23], [24]. Their tasks and mechanisms differ from our binary sensor setting. An architectural analogy does not establish shared statistical behavior or biological equivalence.

Outcome preferences, reward representations, and action selection

The difference between specifying outcome preferences and specifying rewards is useful, but it is not an absolute division between inspectable and opaque objectives. If preferences are encoded as pC(o)∝exp(βr(o)), the expected log-preference term corresponds to expected reward up to a scale and normalization constant. Epistemic value adds a particular information-seeking objective. Active inference and model-based reinforcement learning have operational connections [25], [26]; both require assumptions about observations, actions, and values.

Our question is therefore comparative: which explicit reliability or monitoring mechanism changes behavior under a specified environment and resource budget? The four labels do not determine the answer in advance.

AI safety, information integrity, and evidential vulnerability

Objective specification, distribution shift, and oversight are established concerns in AI safety [27], [28]. We use the quadrants to organize where an intervention acts: on observations, metadata, monitoring rules, or model revision. We do not infer human susceptibility, attack success probabilities, or alignment guarantees from the taxonomy. The misleading-metadata control is a controlled synthetic failure case, not a deployment security evaluation.

Bayesian model criticism and the resource limits of inquiry

Gelman and Shalizi distinguish inference within a model from criticism and revision of the model itself [29]. That distinction motivates our separation of posterior updating from the evaluation of its assumptions. Simon's bounded account of rational choice makes resource limitations relevant to a decision procedure [30]; accordingly, an accuracy improvement obtained with extra observations is evaluated alongside its cost.

Proper scoring rules provide a principled basis for assessing probabilistic forecasts [31]. We use Brier and logarithmic losses in addition to classification accuracy. These losses assess forecast quality; neither is a pure calibration statistic. Descriptive calibration diagrams separately compare forecast probabilities with held-out frequencies, following the distinction between coherence and calibration [32].

Out-of-sample predictive assessment and the meanings of calibration

Bayesian workflow treats model construction, checking, computation, and comparison as linked activities rather than reducing scientific practice to a posterior calculation [33]. This supplies a methodological neighbor for the four roles: each role identifies a place where inquiry can change course, while workflow specifies how a sequence of such changes is evaluated. Our synthetic study implements one held-out evaluation cycle; it does not establish convergence of an open-ended revision process.

Predictive evaluation must follow the sampling structure. Demšar's comparisons concern algorithms across multiple datasets [34]; our seed replicates are generated datasets within one environment, not independently chosen scientific domains. Thus, seed uncertainty cannot stand in for cross-domain generalization. Vehtari and colleagues analyze out-of-sample predictive evaluation with LOO and WAIC [35]. We use a separately generated test set and frozen fitted reliabilities; we do not claim to implement those estimators or their diagnostics.

The word calibration also needs an explicit object. Guo and colleagues examine confidence calibration in neural classifiers [36], whereas our curves assess probabilities of state 1 in a binary generative task. Vaicenavicius and colleagues give a broader framework for evaluating probabilistic calibration [37]. Their distinctions motivate treating a binned reliability curve as a diagnostic with finite-sample and binning limitations. Simulation-based calibration instead checks an inference algorithm across generative draws [38]. Our algebraic VFE/EFE identities and held-out forecast curves are different checks and should not be relabeled as simulation-based calibration.

Resources and the scope of sensitivity analysis

Gigerenzer and Gaissmaier's ecological approach to heuristics makes strategy quality conditional on the structure of the decision environment [39]. This sharpens the interpretation of our stopping rule: a rule can economize on observations in this task without becoming a generally rational procedure. Our comparison does not establish which heuristics people use, nor does it estimate an ecological optimum.

Resource-rational analysis asks how computational constraints enter an account of cognitive performance [40]. Our observation counts operationalize one resource; they do not measure cognitive effort, time, or energy. The comparison therefore supports a resource accounting argument while leaving the mapping to biological mechanisms unresolved.

Steegen and colleagues make analytical alternatives visible through multiverse analysis [41]. Our threshold sweep follows that transparency motivation in a narrower setting: eleven explicit acquisition thresholds reuse the same test trials. It is a sensitivity analysis, not an exhaustive multiverse or an independent validation of a selected policy. Any threshold chosen from this sweep would need a new evaluation set.

Situated inquiry and the origins of commitments

Dewey's treatment of the reflex arc emphasizes coordinated activity rather than an isolated stimulus followed by an isolated response [42]. This motivates asking how observation and action jointly define a task. Our action model implements one small, explicit coupling: an intervention changes the observation mode while preserving the hidden context. It does not reproduce Dewey's account of organismic coordination.

Friedman's Before Pragmatism Had a Name develops a reading of Blake alongside pragmatist inquiry and active inference [43]. We draw a bounded methodological lesson from this conceptual juxtaposition: an inherited representational habit can itself become an object of inquiry. The historical and literary synthesis is an attributed interpretation, not empirical support for the present taxonomy or a mathematical equivalence between Blake's figures and generative-model variables.

Synthesis of Agent and Niche stages a speculative encounter between Blake and ecological accounts of cognition [44]. It directs attention toward the setting that makes an observation meaningful. Here that attention becomes a testable modeling distinction between a declared agent and a separately specified world. The literary work supplies conceptual context, whereas the retained executable specifications and scores supply evidence for the numerical comparison.

Prior Cognitive Art asks which explanatory question is being answered when the origin or persistence of a prior is invoked [45]. Its conceptual organization draws on Tinbergen's questions about causation, development, survival value, and evolution [46]. A fitted probability, a developmental account, and an evolutionary explanation are therefore different explanatory objects. Our fixed categorical prior is an input to a calculation. Neither a successful posterior update nor the addition of a hyperprior explains its biological origin. The cited working paper presents conceptual and deterministic illustrations, not stochastic performance evidence for our study.

Research economy and public representations

Beni and Pietarinen connect active inference with Peirce's abduction, induction and economy of research [47]. Their philosophical alignment is a close antecedent to the present interpretation. Our narrower contribution is to expose concrete commitments and their evaluation through retained executable models. Selecting an informative action within a fixed representation is not equivalent to inventing a representation; reducing prediction error alone does not authorize an unrestricted change of ends.

Diagrams also participate in scientific accountability. A visualization can make assumptions, units and comparisons inspectable, or conceal them through scale, typography and omission. Rougier and colleagues treat figure design as communication between data and readers [48]. Here the conceptual quadrant map, exact model surfaces and seed-based estimates have different captions and visual roles. Readability does not turn a conceptual diagram into evidence, and numerical precision does not make a model adequate. A public representation should expose the distinction.

Executable specification as an inspectable scientific artifact

The GNN project defines a structured notation and parsing pipeline for active-inference generative models [49]. We import its reference Markdown parser at a recorded revision and consume the resulting parameter values. Our adapter admits a bounded, single-factor categorical profile. GNN is the specification dependency; the local exact categorical equations are the execution engine. No claim is made that every upstream backend, model family, or parser acceptance condition is supported by this adapter.

Executable Generative Models of Evidence, Decisions, and Action

A generative model expresses how hidden conditions could produce observations and how actions could change what happens next. Active inference uses that model both to interpret evidence and to evaluate possible action. The crucial distinction is between a world that generates an observation and an agent's assumptions about that world. An agent can execute its assumptions precisely while misunderstanding the reliability of what it observes.

Two experimental models and their distinct explanatory questions

Figure fig:executed_models separates the two experimental settings. The sensor study asks how beliefs change when the reliability of an observation is ignored, correctly supplied, learned or falsely reported. Its hidden context remains fixed during a trial. Several sensors offer conditionally independent views of that context; acquiring another view consumes an explicit resource. A confidence-triggered acquisition rule is a monitoring mechanism, not an EFE policy.

The action study asks a different question: when does an agent choose an informative observation? Its hidden state combines context with observation mode. Actions preserve context while choosing between a blind mode, which returns a null observation, and an informative mode, which provides a noisy indication of context. Expected free energy evaluates the consequences of these actions under declared preferences as well as their information value. Neither the preference specification nor a successful calculation establishes which ends should be adopted.

Executable declarations make modeling commitments inspectable

The models declare observation likelihoods, action-dependent transitions, preferences and initial beliefs in real GNN specifications. The upstream reference parser supplies the tensors consumed by the local categorical runtime. This connects a readable declaration to the calculation actually performed; it does not certify the declaration's truth. The supplementary tensor atlas, Figure fig:canonical_model_atlas, also shows the packaged stay/switch control model used for numerical checks. It is distinct from the two retained experimental tasks.

Bayesian updating revises beliefs conditional on a model. Expected free energy evaluates prospective information and preferred outcomes within that model [10], [11]. Criticism asks whether the model deserves continued use [29]. Keeping these activities distinct prevents an exact posterior from becoming a certificate of adequate representation. Testing Model Commitments Through Controlled Synthetic Studies

The framework becomes scientifically useful when its roles identify interventions with observable consequences. The studies therefore compare concrete mechanisms rather than treating the quadrant names as algorithms. Their design is synthetic, fixed in code and not preregistered. No participant data or deployment outcomes are analyzed. Complete task parameters, randomization, statistical procedures and retained-file definitions are in Supplement Complete synthetic experimental design and statistical protocol.

Source reliability, evidence acquisition, and supervised learning

The sensor study compares a fixed reliability assumption with source-specific metadata, confidence-triggered acquisition and reliability learned from separate labeled training. Every strategy shares the same held-out contexts and potential sensor observations within each seed. A misleading-metadata control tests whether contextual information can make inference worse. Fixed sensing budgets distinguish the effects of additional evidence from those of a stopping rule.

Figure fig:quadrant_experiment shows why a single ranking would obscure the argument. Reliable metadata improves a misspecified baseline in this environment; further evidence can improve predictions while consuming more observations. Fitting reliability is an externally supervised intervention and carries a training cost. Falsely reported metadata can undermine a mathematically valid update. These are consequences of explicit model choices, not evidence that higher-order processing is intrinsically superior.

Action selection under explicit preferences and model assumptions

The separate action task samples the policy probabilities actually returned by EFE evaluation. Always-inspect and always-blind baselines share the generated contexts and potential informative outcomes. Preferences can make an uninformative action attractive; a policy can therefore sacrifice classification accuracy without violating its declared objective. The mechanism is inspected as an example of how information and value enter action, rather than as a universal prescription for sensing.

An exact comparison then fixes the agent's assumptions while varying the world's actual reliability. Each side has a separately imported GNN specification. Forecasts are evaluated using world probabilities, so a confident but mistaken likelihood becomes visible without attributing Monte Carlo noise to the discrepancy. This separation is essential to model criticism: the model's own expectations cannot be its only tribunal.

Experimental units, uncertainty, and the scope of the evidence

Independent generated seeds are the unit of uncertainty. Paired seed estimates, proper scoring losses, observation counts and prespecified adverse controls provide complementary views of each mechanism. Training data remain separate from held-out evaluation. Bootstrap intervals are marginal; the primary sign-flip tests retain their stated exchangeability assumption and three-comparison Holm family [50], [51]. Exploratory sensitivity analyses introduce no new confirmatory rejection claims.

The exact reliability grid is finite enumeration, not a collection of empirical replications. Its curves receive neither sampling intervals nor p-values. Probabilistic scores assess forecast quality, while calibration examines agreement between forecasts and outcomes [31], [32]. Computational checks, synthetic behavior and philosophical interpretation support different conclusions; none substitutes for the others.

Four Complementary Roles in Meta-Pragmatic Inquiry

The two conceptual axes and four operational responsibilities

The horizontal axis distinguishes observations from information about their production, reliability, or provenance. The vertical axis distinguishes performing inference from monitoring, evaluating, or revising inference. A datum can be metadata relative to one task and ordinary data relative to another; classification must therefore specify the object-level task and monitored process.

Data Metadata
Object-level cognition Q1: infer states and evaluate actions Q2: condition inference on source reliability or context
Meta-cognitive regulation Q3: monitor uncertainty and alter processing or acquisition Q4: evaluate or revise the reliability model, strategy, or representation

The axes are analytically distinguishable, but we do not assert statistical independence or universal neural separability. One implementation can contain operations assigned to several quadrants. Neither a timestamp nor a confidence value alone makes a system meta-cognitive; there must be a specified monitoring/control relation. The OODA comparison in Section OODA, active inference, and criticism across the action loop makes the same distinction: these responsibilities cut across a perception--action loop rather than naming four successive stages.

The early map in Figure fig:quadrant_orientation provides the shared orientation for these roles. A complementary revision-cycle diagram appears in Supplement Model revision mechanics and the limits of integrity diagnostics, Figure fig:model_revision_map.

Q1: infer hidden states and evaluate actions within a model

Q1 includes Equation (1) and policy evaluation in Equation (3) under a fixed model. The experimental Q1 baseline uses the same inference rule as the other conditions, with a homogeneous reliability assumption. Its weakness is model misspecification, rather than the absence of any Bayesian operation. Correctly specified Q1 inference can match a metadata-aware strategy when metadata carries no additional information.

Q2: contextualize evidence through source reliability

Q2 uses metadata m by conditioning the likelihood, p(o∣s,m). In the sensor example, m identifies sensor-specific reliability. This changes the evidence supplied to the same Bayesian update. The gain is conditional on the metadata being informative and trustworthy. If metadata is irrelevant, unavailable, or false, it may yield no gain or cause harm.

Alternative weighted decision objectives require their own derivation and justification; the supplementary discussion distinguishes such heuristics from normalized EFE.

Q3: monitor uncertainty and regulate evidence acquisition

Q3 observes a property of the inference process and changes what the system does next. Here, maximum posterior probability determines whether another observation is acquired. The monitoring rule is bounded by an explicit sensing budget. The mechanism improves accuracy when new observations are sufficiently informative, but incurs cost and can fail under miscalibrated beliefs.

Posterior entropy and maximum posterior probability describe belief concentration. Calibration requires comparison with realized outcomes. The package's composite confidence and attention examples are illustrative control rules; their weights are not fitted psychological parameters or calibrated probabilities of correctness.

Q4: evaluate model assumptions and justify their revision

Q4 evaluates the reliability model and changes its parameters. The implemented mechanism estimates likelihood reliabilities on a separate labeled training set and freezes them before test. This is model-level adaptation under external supervision. It is not unrestricted self-modification, a learned ontology, or evidence that an agent can independently choose appropriate goals.

A general framework-revision objective may combine predictive fit, resource cost, and constraints on permissible changes. The choice of those terms requires justification; lowering an in-sample objective is not enough. Held-out evaluation and bounded update rules distinguish improved fit from overfitting or drift. Revising preferences also requires an external criterion for whether the revision is desirable.

Overlapping roles, comparative predictions, and falsifiability

The framework suggests testable contrasts: informative versus uninformative metadata, matched versus mismatched models, fixed versus adaptive sensing under comparable cost, and training-based revision versus held-out controls. A useful taxonomy should help define these contrasts without predetermining their outcomes. Evidence against a particular mechanism should remain possible. The current study gives one tractable example; broader tasks, correlated sensors, nonstationarity, and representation learning are future tests.

Evidence Integrity, Source Trust, and Model Vulnerability

A scoped threat model for evidence and model commitments

We consider an attacker able to change observations, reported reliability, monitoring rules, or selected model parameters in a categorical artificial agent. The assets are the fidelity of state estimates, the integrity of preferences, and the validity of model revisions. We assume the evaluation state and held-out scoring code remain trusted. This is a conceptual and synthetic threat model; it does not establish attack prevalence, impact in deployed systems, or human cognitive vulnerability.

Intervention Example Evidence or limitation
Observations (Q1) Alter a sensor outcome Can be studied as a controlled observation perturbation
Metadata (Q2) Overstate reliability of a weak sensor Measured adverse control in the binary task
Monitoring (Q3) Alter a confidence threshold or sensing budget Requires a separate monitoring-rule intervention
Model revision (Q4) Poison training labels or permit preference drift Requires a trusted update reference and held-out evaluation

These locations need not be exclusive. Altering a likelihood matrix may affect both direct inference and metadata-conditioned inference. There is no measured monotonic ranking of danger or detectability across Q1--Q4.

Misleading metadata as a measured synthetic failure case

The misleading-metadata condition claims high reliability for the weakest sensor and low reliability for the strongest. Held-out accuracy falls to 54.12%, compared with 84.68% under truthful reliability metadata. Log loss also increases. This demonstrates a specific misspecification failure under known ground truth. It does not establish the effectiveness of a detector, defense, or real-world influence operation.

Trust, calibration, and the limits of diagnosis

An anomaly score can reveal a discrepancy without identifying its cause. A change may be benign, and a harmful intervention may remain close to the baseline. Likewise, a well-formed probability model can encode false information. The technical diagnostic contracts are retained in Supplement Model revision mechanics and the limits of integrity diagnostics; neither their thresholds nor the taxonomy establish calibrated security performance.

Bounded integrity safeguards and unresolved assurance questions

A practical system can record trusted parameter versions, verify input provenance, constrain allowed revisions, retain rollback points, and test candidate changes on held-out data. These are design proposals requiring separate evaluation. Model hashes establish byte identity against a trusted reference; they do not establish the truth or appropriateness of that reference.

Preference specification remains a normative choice. An inspectable C vector can help make that choice discussable, but its mathematical representation does not ensure alignment with human values [28], [27]. Future security experiments should state attacker capabilities, clean and attacked distributions, detector calibration data, resource budgets, and false-positive/false-negative tradeoffs. No security efficacy claim is made here.

The separate claims an assurance argument must establish

Specification provenance addresses which model was executed. Mathematical checks address whether the declared calculation obeys its contracts. Held-out controls address how that mechanism behaves in a specified environment. None answers the normative question whether the objective should be adopted. A defensible assurance argument states the trusted reference, permitted interventions, evaluation distribution, adoption criterion, and known failures separately. The misleading-metadata condition illustrates why a well-formed specification can still encode bad information.

Interpreting Experimental Evidence and Justifying Model Revision

What the controlled experiments establish about these mechanisms

The experiment supports a narrow conclusion: using accurate source reliability can improve a misspecified inference baseline, and additional evidence can improve accuracy at additional cost. Estimating reliability from labeled training approximates the oracle condition in this task. These findings are compatible with standard Bayesian model specification and sequential evidence acquisition. They do not require that the four quadrants correspond to distinct computational or biological faculties.

Q2 and Q4 produce identical held-out classification accuracy in the retained run, while their Brier and log losses differ. In this parameter regime, fitted likelihood weights preserve the ordering of classification decisions across seeds. Equal accuracy therefore does not imply identical beliefs or perfect parameter recovery. Conversely, a sizable standardized seed-level effect largely reflects small variation in replicate means, so absolute percentage-point differences and sensing costs deserve priority.

What the four-role taxonomy contributes to model criticism

The taxonomy makes three questions explicit: what evidence is modeled, what information is available about its reliability, and which part of processing can be monitored or revised. It connects model specification to testable interventions and makes assumptions inspectable. It does not supersede established work on learning, monitoring/control, active learning, or hierarchical inference.

Changing A, B, C, or D has different meanings: observation model revision, dynamics revision, preference revision, and prior revision. These should not all be treated as the same form of optimization. Empirical fit can assess some epistemic revisions, whereas preference revision additionally needs a justified normative reference.

Meta-epistemic practice as fallible inquiry

The philosophical proposal is methodological: model specification should be open to criticism through its consequences. Inference is conditional on a representation; inquiry includes asking whether that representation excludes a relevant possibility. Gelman and Shalizi's account of model checking and revision supplies a useful precedent for this distinction [29]. Our taxonomy does not resolve the disagreement between confirmation-based and criticism-based philosophies of Bayesian practice. It makes the distinction visible in an executable case.

Metadata has no intrinsic privilege. Reliability information becomes evidence only through a model of its source, relevance, and trustworthiness. A posterior can be exact conditional on false metadata. GNN makes that conditioning inspectable; it does not eliminate the need for independent evidence about the source. Fallibility therefore applies to the likelihood, its reliability estimate, the monitoring rule, and the evaluator's own reference.

Meta-pragmatic practice and the authority to revise

Preference logits specify a ranking of outcomes, not a complete ethics. An informational objective, a sensing budget, and an adoption threshold embody different judgments. Our interpretation connects the evaluation of those judgments to James's emphasis on practical consequences [4], without equating usefulness with unconstrained truth or morality. Better prediction under one scoring rule cannot authorize a change in whose interests the system serves.

The modeler and the modeled agent must also be distinguished. Here the researcher declares the task, external labels supervise learning, and a fixed rule triggers sensing. The experiment does not show an agent independently inventing a representation or legitimating its own ends. An autonomous revision mechanism would additionally need an admissible change space, an independent evaluation reference, and a rule for accepting or rejecting the change. Those requirements are design proposals.

Comparing prediction quality, sensing resources, and calibration

The fixed-budget comparators make the resource tradeoff explicit. Their results should be read together with Q3's stopping rule and average cost; a single accuracy ranking does not identify an optimal procedure. A decision about whether extra observations are worthwhile needs a stated cost function. No wall-clock, energy, or monetary performance is inferred from observation counts. This connects the computational comparison to bounded rationality while leaving application-specific valuations open [30].

In the retained run, fixed four-observation inference achieves 86.75% accuracy and fixed five-observation inference achieves 90.74%. Q3 achieves 90.34% at a mean 3.892 observations. The stopping rule allocates observations to less confident trials; it can therefore outperform a fixed four-observation schedule while using fewer observations on average. These are descriptive results in one design, not a claim of universal dominance or a new multiplicity-adjusted comparison. Fixed five-observation inference has lower retained Brier and logarithmic losses than Q3, so similar classification accuracy does not mean that early stopping preserves all probabilistic information.

Probabilistic quality is likewise multidimensional. Accuracy discards the strength of a belief, proper losses penalize probability errors, and the reliability diagram displays empirical agreement with predicted probabilities [31], [32]. The diagram is descriptive for this task and binning scheme; it is not a guarantee under distribution shift.

Bröcker and Smith show why bin populations, forecast distributions and sampling variation complicate the visual reading of reliability diagrams [52]. Figure fig:resource_calibration therefore separates comparisons and exposes bin occupancy through point area. Its pooled lines remain descriptive. We do not implement their consistency-resampling procedure, and a visually smooth curve or a small distance from the diagonal is not a statistical calibration test.

Stopping inquiry requires an explicit resource-sensitive judgment

The stopping rule expresses a procedural commitment about when present confidence warrants ending observation. Extra evidence may improve prediction while demanding resources; a resource saving does not by itself establish an acceptable error cost. Simon's bounded rationality and resource-rational analysis motivate evaluating this tradeoff rather than hiding it in a single performance score [30], [40].

The detailed paired contrasts and threshold sweep remain in Supplement Paired contrasts, stopping rules, and policy surfaces, Figures fig:paired_diagnostics and fig:stopping_sensitivity. They expose analytical choices without selecting and validating a new policy on the same trials.

Always inspecting obtains more informative evidence, while always remaining blind supplies an explicit limiting case. The distinction links an executable policy to the philosophical question of who may authorize its commitments.

Exact inference and confident error in a misspecified world

Figure fig:model_robustness separates the agent's model from the world's likelihood. At actual reliability 0.50, inspection is uninformative. The agent nevertheless reports inspection posteriors of 0.15 or 0.85 because it retains its assumed reliability of 0.85. Its EFE action probabilities also remain fixed. Always inspecting then has exact Brier loss 0.3725, worse than the blind baseline's 0.25, although both classify at chance. This is a proper-score failure hidden by equal accuracy. The case establishes a consequence of the specified mismatch, not a universal argument against information-seeking action.

The practical response requires evidence about reliability, a permitted update, and fresh evaluation of its consequences. Recomputing an exact posterior under the same incorrect likelihood does not provide that evidence. This is the meta-pragmatic distinction in executable form: action selection follows declared commitments, while criticism asks what would warrant retaining or changing them. The analysis holds the agent fixed to reveal the failure; it does not implement autonomous detection or repair of misspecification.

Limitations of the synthetic design and philosophical interpretation

The task has two states, conditionally independent sensors, fixed reliabilities, and no changing state within a trial. Labels are available for training Q4, and true reliability is supplied to Q2/Q3. Q3 receives a larger sensing budget. Those assumptions favor the tested mechanisms; correlated observations, incorrect metadata, changing reliability, and costly acquisition can alter the result.

The original sensor experiment evaluates state estimation and confidence-triggered sensing; its gains are not attributed to EFE. The separate action task does execute EFE-based policy sampling and Bayesian context inference, but it is a one-step categorical control example with known dynamics and likelihood. Neither task establishes long-horizon, continuous, hierarchical or representation-learning performance. The package's narrative framework/modeler classes and schematic FEP visualizations remain educational demonstrations.

Confidence intervals concern this simulation design. Bootstrap seed resampling approximates variability over independent generated datasets, not uncertainty about all possible environments. Sign-flip tests require a symmetric/exchangeable null; they are supplementary to retained estimates and model assumptions. The three primary tests use Holm correction, while the displayed 95% intervals are marginal. No retrospective power calculation or fabricated regression fit is reported.

The manuscript is a working research document, without claimed peer review or independent replication. Bibliographic corrections are documented separately; bibliography links establish source identity, not independent confirmation of every interpretation. Synthetic failures do not support clinical or deployment guidance.

From controlled synthetic examples to independent empirical tests

Useful next tests would compare equal sensing budgets, include model-free and model-based decision baselines, introduce correlated and shifting sensor noise, vary training-label availability, and evaluate metadata provenance or corruption detectors on held-out attacks. Representation-changing Q4 mechanisms require complexity penalties and external evaluation. A biological study would need measured behavior, confidence reports, a fitted model, and appropriate metacognitive measures [22], rather than mapping the quadrant labels directly onto neural structures.

Toward Accountable Active Inference Through Public Criticism

Active inference makes observation likelihoods, dynamics, preferences, and priors explicit modeling objects. We interpret decisions about these objects as meta-epistemic and meta-pragmatic activities and organize examples with a data/metadata by cognition/meta-cognition taxonomy. The taxonomy is useful when it clarifies a mechanism, a comparison, and a possible failure, rather than when it merely assigns a higher-order label.

The implementation exposes explicit probability conventions, categorical and virtual-evidence inference, normalized EFE decompositions, exact variational bounds and reproducible experiment artifacts. In the specified binary sensor task, reliability-aware inference improves accuracy over a homogeneous-reliability baseline, adaptive sensing improves accuracy with additional observations, and supervised reliability fitting approximates an oracle model. Misleading metadata causes a substantial failure. These outcomes delimit the claim: better-specified or better-informed models can help, but monitoring and revision require resources, evidence and constraints.

The next research step is to test those mechanisms under richer environments and matched budgets. The present revision contributes executable GNN sources, an actual upstream parser import, fixed-budget comparators, and separate views of forecast quality and sensing resources. It supplies an inspectable starting point and an audit trail, without claiming universal performance benefits, biological validation, or security guarantees.

The separate GNN action task directly connects the declared state dynamics and likelihood to EFE policy sampling and Bayesian context inference. Its exact preference/information crossover and retained baseline comparisons show why an active inference policy need not maximize classification accuracy alone. This is a bounded demonstration of an executed mechanism; known model parameters, one-step policies and researcher-specified preferences remain substantive limitations.

The exact mismatch analysis adds a further requirement: distinguish the model's expected consequences from consequences in a separately specified world. Fixed beliefs can yield mathematically correct EFE choices and poor probabilistic predictions when the likelihood is wrong. The retained calibration cells and independent score identities make that limitation inspectable.

Active inference serves as a meta-pragmatic framework when a declared model, executable action procedure, external evaluation reference, and accountable revision process are brought together. The central practical commitment is to expose what can be challenged: the representation of evidence, the authority of metadata, the cost of acquisition, and the criterion for model revision. The terminology alone does not provide that discipline.

Categorical probability contracts and exact Bayesian inference

The discrete generative model and its tensor conventions

Let s index hidden states, o observations, and a actions. The implementation uses Ao,s=p(o∣s), Bs′,s,a=p(s′∣s,a), finite outcome preference logits Co, and a normalized initial prior Ds. Columns of A and of each action slice of B sum to one. A two-dimensional B is promoted to a single-action tensor. Arrays with incompatible dimensions, empty axes, negative probabilities, nonfinite values, or invalid normalization are rejected.

The normalized outcome preference is pC(o)=exp(Co)/∑jexp(Cj). Logits are not probabilities; adding a constant to all entries leaves the normalized preference unchanged. Model validation copies inputs so that later mutation of caller-owned arrays does not silently change an initialized model. Public model arrays remain inspectable and mutable; callers must validate deliberate revisions.

The supported GNN profile and executable import boundary

Canonical GNN model files live under src/core/gnn_models/. The binary-sensor specification declares static identity dynamics; the stay-switch agent declares two action-conditioned transition slices. Both declare the same axis conventions used by the runtime. Each numerical intervention is materialized as GNN Markdown, parsed by the actual upstream MarkdownGNNParser, checked against this project's supported profile, and converted into the matrices consumed by inference.

The adapter rejects missing or duplicate sections, parser warnings, unsupported flags and model families, contradictory dimensions, malformed tensors, invalid probabilities, and unsupported dependency graphs. It verifies the upstream Git origin, pinned revision, and tracked source state before importing the parser. An absent checkout does not select a substitute parser or an undeclared model. Tensor changes are tested through changes in executed beliefs and policy choices, not merely through successful parsing.

This boundary makes the specification executable without confusing syntax validity with scientific adequacy. The experiment retains the exact materialized sensor specifications and their digests. Random-model identity checks and the reliability sweep also pass through GNN import. Direct tensor constructors remain available as a low-level numerical API and as independent test oracles.

Bayesian state inference with hard and virtual evidence

For a categorical observation, Bayes' rule gives:

q(s∣o)=Ao,sDs∑jAo,jDj.

For a normalized nonnegative virtual-evidence vector e(o), the implementation uses Le(s)=∑oe(o)Ao,s and replaces Ao,s with Le(s) in Equation (1). This convention models evidence about possible outcomes. It differs from a Jeffrey update, which mixes already-conditioned posteriors. A uniform evidence vector is uninformative because each likelihood column sums to one. Zero-probability states retain zero mass; evidence impossible under the model and prior raises an error instead of being repaired by clipping.

Exact variational free energy and the evidence bound

Given an explicit joint distribution and a normalized candidate posterior q, variational free energy is:

F(q,o)=∑sq(s)[logq(s)−logp(o,s)]=−logp(o)+DKL[q(s)‖p(s∣o)].

Equation (2) bounds negative log evidence and reaches the bound at the exact posterior. Candidate beliefs assigning positive mass outside posterior support have infinite free energy. The exact implementation is calculate_variational_free_energy; the older model-free FreeEnergyPrinciple.calculate_free_energy path remains an explicitly labeled schematic score for compatibility. Its trajectories and state-boundary diagrams do not establish a variational bound, conditional independence, or the physical claims of the FEP. Supplying a generative model to that class selects the exact calculation.

Normalized expected free energy and categorical policy selection

For each future policy step, propagate qτ=B(aτ)qτ−1 and predict qo=Aqτ. With an exact conditional update, state information gain is I(s;o)=H(qo)−∑sqτ(s)H(A:,s). Our normalized planning score is:

Gτ=−I(s;o∣π)−∑oqo(o)logpC(o)=DKL[qo‖pC]+∑sqτ(s)H(A:,s).

The equivalence in Equation (3) follows by expanding mutual information and KL divergence. It is exact for this finite, known-likelihood predictive model. It does not include uncertainty about parameters, posterior approximation error, or observation-contingent replanning.

For compatibility, the returned total uses the raw-logit score −I−𝔼[C]. normalized_total adds logsumexp(C) per step and equals risk plus ambiguity. Normalized values are evaluated directly from stable log preferences to avoid cancellation under large constant logit shifts. Policy rankings agree mathematically for a fixed model and horizon convention. Mean aggregation is the legacy default; sum aggregation implements additive finite-horizon costs. Comparing different horizons or different preference models requires specifying this convention and normalization explicitly.

Policy selection accepts current posterior beliefs, with D used when beliefs are omitted. It returns an argmin and a policy distribution proportional to exp(−γG), where γ>0 is precision. Increasing precision concentrates this distribution; it does not change an exact argmin. Equal scores retain a uniform distribution. All entropy and KL quantities use natural logarithms and are measured in nats.

The binary sensor task and its resource-matched controls

Each test trial independently draws a binary hidden state from a uniform prior. Five conditionally independent binary sensors agree with that state with probabilities (0.55,0.65,0.85,0.80,0.80). Every strategy sees the same pre-generated test states and observations within a seed. The first three sensors are the common initial evidence. The state remains fixed within a trial and resets independently between trials.

  • Q1 fixed: inference assumes reliability 0.80 for each of the first three sensors.
  • Q2 metadata: inference uses the true, sensor-specific reliabilities for the same three observations. This is an oracle-metadata condition.
  • Q3 adaptive: starts from Q2, then acquires a fourth and, if needed, fifth observation while the maximum posterior probability is below 0.90. Its cost is the actual observation count, with a maximum of five.
  • Q4 learned: estimates the first three reliabilities from 200 separate labeled training trials per seed, using a Beta(1,1) prior. Parameters are frozen before held-out evaluation. It uses exactly three test observations.
  • Misleading metadata: uses claimed reliabilities (0.95,0.85,0.55) for the first three sensors. This prespecified adverse control reverses the ordering of sensor quality.
  • Fixed four / fixed five: use true reliability with exactly four or five observations on every trial. These added descriptive comparators separate a fixed sensing budget from the confidence-triggered stopping rule. They are additions to this revision, not preregistered tests.

Q3 changes available evidence, whereas Q4 changes fitted likelihood parameters. They are not matched to Q1 on every resource. We report these differences rather than attributing all gains to a generic meta-level capacity. The confidence score controlling Q3 is maximum posterior probability; it is not the composite heuristic returned by MetaCognitiveSystem.

An action task driven by expected free energy

A separate exploratory task directly executes active inference action selection. Four hidden states encode binary context crossed with observation mode: two blind-mode states and two informative-mode states. Two actions preserve context while setting the mode. The blind action produces a null outcome; inspection produces one of two context-indicating outcomes with reliability r=0.85. The declared likelihood is

A=(110000r1−r001−rr),C=(c,0,0),D=(1/2,1/2,0,0),c=0.15.

Each transition slice deterministically maps the current context to the action's mode. Every model is materialized as GNN and parsed through the actual upstream reference parser before the local categorical runtime consumes its tensors. Thus, increasing the preference for the null outcome can reverse the minimum-EFE action even when information quality is fixed. Algebraic derivation checks and analytical reference cases

Expanding DKL(qo‖pC) gives −H(qo)−E[logpC]. Adding ambiguity EqsH(A:,s) gives −I(s;o)−E[logpC], establishing Equation (3). Replacing normalized log preferences with logits subtracts log∑oexp(Co) per policy step. This constant affects reported magnitudes but not a fixed-model, fixed-convention policy ranking.

For VFE, substitute p(o,s)=p(s∣o)p(o) into Equation (2). The log-evidence term factors out of the normalized expectation, leaving posterior KL divergence. With q=p(s∣o) the divergence is zero. Exact-zero handling uses 0log0=0 and retains infinite divergences for support violations.

Tests additionally compare batched binary log-odds inference with sequential core Bayesian updates, check asymmetric column-stochastic transitions, and check preference-shift and horizon-aggregation identities. Mathematical identity tests complement software coverage; coverage alone does not establish theoretical correctness.

Modular implementation and asymptotic computational complexity

core defines categorical models and exact inference. framework supplies the taxonomy, monitoring illustrations, and structural security diagnostics. analysis contains generators, statistics, validation, experiments, and pipeline orchestration. visualization creates educational and experiment figures; utils supplies manuscript validation and provenance. No parent repository infrastructure is required by the research pipeline.

For a dense model with Ns states, No outcomes, Na available actions and a policy of T steps, a single policy costs approximately O(T(Ns2+NoNs)) for transitions and predictions, with O(NoNs) likelihood entropy preprocessing. Evaluating Nπ candidate policies multiplies the per-policy cost by Nπ. The available-action count identifies the transition tensor's third axis; it is not an additional factor when evaluating a fixed sequence. The implementation does not claim sparse scaling or measured support for 1,000-state models. No runtime table is reported without a retained benchmark protocol and hardware description.

Conceptual terms and epistemic boundaries

Q1–Q4 name inferential, contextual, monitoring and revision roles in Figure fig:quadrant_orientation; they are not measured natural cognitive kinds. Metadata is information about the source, production or reliability of task-level evidence. Its role depends on the task relation.

Monitoring evaluates a property of inference; control changes processing on that basis. Calibration concerns agreement between probabilistic forecasts and observed frequencies, rather than posterior concentration. Proper losses measure forecast quality and need not be pure calibration statistics.

Meta-epistemic practice examines the representation, acquisition and reliability of evidence. Meta-pragmatic practice examines action, preferred consequences, budgets and the criteria authorizing revision. Structural integrity denotes validity of numerical contracts; it does not establish truthful metadata, absence of tampering or legitimacy of preferences.
